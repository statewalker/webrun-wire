An exchange on a windowed `multiplexPort` port takes one round trip, in both directions, instead of two. The window handshake (`open`, the responder's zero-byte ack, the opener's answer to it) used to hold each end to confirmed calls until it had seen the other's step: a responder pipelined only 1.5 round trips after the port opened (a GET's body came a round trip after its head), and an opener only once the ack came back (a POST's end waited for its first chunk's confirmation). Nothing new goes on the wire; old and new peers interoperate in all four pairings.

- **Responder:** it holds its own sends back by the window from the `open` on, without waiting for the opener's answer, when the `open` lists `caps` (only a mux that grants credit lists them) and this mux has already read a `credit` envelope on any port (which shows its own codec reads them; one that drops them keeps waiting for the answer, unwindowed, as before). A 0.9 or 0.10 opener gets a GET's head and body together from its second port on.
- **Opener:** new option `presumeAck` on `multiplexPort` / `overPipe`, `"learned"` (default), `"always"` or `"never"`. A presuming port takes the responder's ack as given when it opens: it holds back by the window at once, agrees `pipeline`, and posts its answer to the ack right behind the `open`, so a 0.9 or 0.10 responder pipelines from the start too. `"learned"` presumes, on every port after the first, the ack this peer already sent on an earlier port of the same mux, once one listed `pipeline`; `"always"` presumes from the first port, for a pipe whose peer is known to be 0.9 or later; `"never"` keeps the 0.10 behaviour. An ack that contradicts the presumption (other sizing, or no `pipeline`) stops presumption on that mux, and fails the port with reason `"protocol"` only if it already posted something. A port refused without an ack (`maxPorts`) or after one (`onPort`) closes as before, having sent at most one window, which the responder drops.

Behaviour change: by default an opener now sends a stream's chunks before the ack from its second port on, so on a refused port it may send up to a window where it sent one chunk. `ports()` still reports `window` only once the ack arrived. Set `presumeAck: "never"` for the previous timing on the opening side.
